# Brief: three fixes for Paris luxury beauty stores

*Data: 16,705 public Google Maps reviews of 14 Paris luxury beauty points of sale (brand boutiques, department stores, Sephora Champs-Élysées, CDG duty free), January 2016 to November 2026. Collected on 22 November 2026 with Apify; for department stores and Dior, only reviews about beauty are kept. Full collection method and limits in the [README](README.md#where-the-data-comes-from).*

## The situation

Paris luxury beauty shoppers are mostly happy: the average real rating across the 14 points of sale is about 4.4 stars. But the gap between the best and worst stores is wide (4.84 for Printemps Haussmann, 3.72 for CDG Terminal 3 duty free), and the gap is explained by service far more than by price or product.

## 1. Remove the rude moment before adding anything else

**Evidence.** A review that mentions rude or unhelpful staff scores about 2.6 stars lower, three times the effect of any other topic. It appears in 26% of all 1–2 star reviews. The words customers use most are "unpleasant experience", "extremely rude" and "completely ignored": the complaint is usually about being ignored or judged, not about a single mistake.

**Where it matters most.** Dior 30 Montaigne (6.0 staff complaints per 100 written reviews), Printemps Maison Beauté (5.8) and Chanel Marais (5.3), against about 0.5 at the two leaders.

**What to do.** Set a greeting standard (every shopper acknowledged within a short time, even when the advisor is busy), and track "ignored" and "rude" complaints monthly per store as a service KPI next to sales.

## 2. Treat visitors as a separate customer journey

**Evidence.** Reviews in English and other languages mention the tax refund about 10 times more often than French reviews, the language spoken 7 to 14 times more and price 2 to 3.5 times more. Advice is praised in 35% of French reviews but only 21% of reviews in other languages: service quality is lost in translation.

**What to do.** Show which advisors speak which languages, explain the tax refund at the till without being asked (the minimum purchase is EUR 100.01 since 2021), and give price comparisons with the visitor's home market where the brand allows it.

## 3. Read the Google rating with care

**Evidence.** Reviews that thank a named sales associate rose from under 1% of reviews before 2020 to about a third in 2026. They are almost always 5 stars and stores often ask for them. Without them, the 2026 rating is 4.45 instead of 4.63, back to the 2019 level. Service did not improve as much as the headline number suggests.

**What to do.** Report a "real rating" without staff-named reviews to management, and use the topics in reviews (rude staff, stock, price, queues) as the early warning, not the star average.

## Also worth watching

- **Stock gaps** cost about 0.9 stars, more than price, and locals punish them most (-1.2 stars vs -0.3 for visitors).
- **CDG Terminal 3** has the highest price complaints in the study (11.7 per 100 written reviews): airport pricing is the first thing to explain there.
